Q3 2026 Flagged Free Workspaces: Conversion Analysis

Source file: Q3 2026 Flagged Free Workspaces with Conversion Outcomes.csv (treated as notion_flagged_accounts_q3_2026.csv)

Part 1: Converted vs not converted

Row counts

The file has 1,200 rows and 22 columns, with no duplicate account IDs.

  • converted_to_paid = True: 60
  • converted_to_paid = False: 1,140

Main finding

Every one of the 60 converters is a company-domain workspace. None of the personal (307), university (147) or K-12 (80) accounts converted.

Universities pull the raw non-converter averages up a lot (746 members on average), so the numeric table shows non-converters two ways: all 1,140, and only the 606 company-domain accounts. The company-only comparison is the fair one.

Data issues to check

  1. current_plan is "Free" for every converter. The column looks like it was captured before the conversion or never updated.
  2. 12 converters have a conversion_date later than their last_active_date. Either the activity snapshot is older than the conversion data, or one of the date fields is wrong.
  3. 21 rows have no industry. 14 of them are company accounts, including 2 converters.

Override: university and k12_school workspaces are capped at 20, whatever their other points add up to.

Why the bands sit where they do

  • Seats get the most weight (30). 90% of company non-converters have 9 seats or fewer, while the middle converter has 54. Size is measured with seats, not total_members.
  • The top band of each other signal starts near the converters' median: 6 teamspaces, 4 integrations, 7 pricing-page visits and 30+ weekly active users. Most company non-converters sit in the bottom one or two bands.
  • The cap is needed because universities have many weekly active users (median 210) and some seats, so without it they would score in the 30s. With it they max out at 20.
  • Personal accounts have no seats and few active users, so they score 7 at most without a cap.

How student_signups is handled

student_signups earns no points. If any workspace with student signups should instead score 0 overall, no score in this file changes: every workspace with student signups is a university or K-12 account, which the cap already holds at 20 or below.

  • The scores split into two groups. No account lands between 50 and 69, so any threshold from 50 to 70 flags the same 66 accounts.
  • 11 converters score under 50 (the lowest is ACC-11032 at 23). They are small: 5 to 8 seats, 1 to 3 teamspaces, few integrations. None of the six signals tells them apart from typical non-converters, so the rules will miss buyers like these.
  • 17 non-converters score 70 or more, including four at 100: ACC-10409, ACC-10663, ACC-10809 and ACC-10832. They look exactly like the accounts that paid, so they are the strongest leads on the list. Some may already be in an open deal.

Caveat: the bands were set using these same 60 converters, so 74% is a best case. Expect the score to do somewhat worse on Q4 accounts it hasn't seen.
